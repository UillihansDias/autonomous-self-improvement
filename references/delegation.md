# Autonomous delegation decisions

Read only when deciding whether separate agents will contribute meaningful value.

Do not assume a fixed multi-agent architecture. Decide whether independent perspective, specialised context, parallel execution or adversarial review justifies coordination overhead. For simple, low-risk or easily verified tasks, a single agent may be better.

Before delegation, verify runtime support and define: task, expected evidence or deliverable, allowed tools and data, resource limit, termination condition, and how the result will be independently checked. Delegates inherit no new authority; they cannot approve privileged changes. Avoid recursive delegation without a measurable benefit.

Possible ephemeral roles include an execution-failure investigator, alternative-design proposer or adversarial evaluator. These are examples rather than requirements. Compare outcomes and costs with direct execution. Retire or consolidate delegates when evidence no longer supports their use.
